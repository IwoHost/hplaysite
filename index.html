The news

Hplay is in beta

v45 “Eli” was the first beta: after forty-four rounds of alpha, Hplay is ready for people who were not there while it was built. v46 “Kleiner” is the same app at a quarter of the size.

Beta means it is good enough to use every day and not yet finished. Here is what that comes to if you are deciding whether to install it.

Most of it works

What you can count on

Playing your music, screen on or off. The library, folders as playlists, search, the lock screen and the notification, the sleep timer, colours and screen looks. These are the parts that get used every day.

Some of it will not

What may go wrong

It has been tried on a few phones, not hundreds, so something may misbehave on yours. The newest parts have had the least time on real phones: the home screen widget, the redrawn vinyl and the apps from Get More. Things can still move or be renamed between versions.

So keep your own copy of your music, and do not treat the player as the only place your playlists live.

Then tell me

How to report a problem

Use the feedback form, in English or Polish, or email hostlabosupport@gmail.com. Say which phone, what you did and what happened. A person reads it and replies. That is what the beta is for.

New in the beta

  • A look of its own. Hplay green, a cream wheel and an amber middle button, with the HostLabo hexagon as the back button. New installs start in these colours, on the Dark screen.
  • Vinyl, redrawn from the groove up. Each record is pressed in its cover’s own colours, with a proper tonearm that lifts when you pause.
  • A home screen widget with buttons, in four sizes, from a single cell to the full width.
  • Store apps that fit in. Apps from Get More follow your screen look and text size, and bring themselves up to date.
  • Click sounds and fonts. Choose how the wheel clicks and which typeface the player is set in.
  • A quarter of the size. About 3.5 MB to download instead of 12.5 MB: the code it never used is gone.
  • Crash reports, if you want them. When something breaks, Hplay offers to send a report. It never includes your music, and nothing goes unless you press Send.

01

What Hplay is

Most music apps want an account, a subscription and a connection. Hplay wants a folder.

You point it at music that is already on your device and it plays it, with a wheel you spin instead of a list you scroll. Cover art, artists, playlists, search, a sleep timer, a vinyl view that spins while the song runs, and five different screen looks. Nothing is uploaded anywhere. There is no sign-in, because there is nothing to sign in to.

It behaves like a music app should on a phone. It holds the lock screen with the cover art on it, puts controls in the notification shade and on the home screen, takes the volume keys, pauses when you pull your headphones out, and keeps playing with the screen off.

What goes online, and when

Nothing about your listening is sent anywhere: no account, no analytics, no telemetry, and your music never leaves the device. It is not a no-network app, though. This is everything it fetches, and the questions below say more.

WhenWhat it fetches
Every time it startsA tag-reading script from a public CDN (cdnjs). Without a connection the built-in reader carries on alone.
Once each time it opensA check with the download site for a newer version. Nothing of yours goes with it, and it can be turned off in Settings › About Hplay.
When you open Get MoreThe catalogue, whatever you choose to install, and a newer copy of any app you installed from it.
When you open Your Year, WrappedIts page on this site. Your numbers ride in the part of the link after the #, which is never sent.
If you tap Buy Me a CoffeeThat site, opened in your phone’s browser.
Only if you press Send Report after a crashA report of what went wrong, sent by email to HostLabo through Web3Forms (the same service as the feedback form): the app version, your phone model and Android version, and the settings. Never your music or the names of your songs. Not Now sends nothing.

Once you have it

Step one

Install it

Open the file you downloaded. Your phone will ask whether you trust an app from outside the store. Say yes, and it installs like any other. Nothing to register, no account to make.

Step two

Add music

Point Hplay at a folder of your own files, or add songs one at a time. It reads the tags and the cover art out of the files themselves.

Step three

Spin the wheel

Drag around the wheel to move, press the middle to choose, press the hexagon at the top to go back. A short guided tour runs the first time so you are not guessing.

06

Questions

What is Hplay?

Hplay is a music player for Android that plays the music files already stored on your phone. Instead of a list you scroll, it is driven by a wheel: you turn the ring with your thumb to move through a list and press the middle to choose. It needs no account, and playing your music needs no connection.

Is the beta safe to install?

Hplay has been in beta since v45. Most of it works: playing music, the library, playlists, the lock screen and the notification are what get used every day. Some of it will not work on your phone, because it has been tried on a few phones and not on hundreds. It only reads your music, it never changes or deletes your files, and it can be uninstalled like any other app. When something goes wrong, the feedback form or an email to hostlabosupport@gmail.com is how it gets fixed.

Does Hplay use the internet?

Not to play your music. Your songs are read from the device and so is the cover art, out of the tags inside the files and not from an online lookup, and nothing about what you play is ever sent anywhere.

But Hplay is not a no-network app, and it would be wrong to say so. It loads one script when it starts, a tag reader from a public CDN, which fills in tags its own reader missed; without a connection that one request fails and the built-in reader carries on alone. It also asks the download site, once each time it opens, whether there is a newer version; nothing of yours goes with the question, and Settings › About Hplay › Check for Updates turns it off. Get More fetches a catalogue when you open it, downloads whatever you choose to install, and brings any app you installed from it up to date. Your Year, Wrapped opens its page on this site; your numbers travel in the part of the link after the #, which a browser never sends, and the covers in it come straight from the app. Tapping Buy Me a Coffee opens that site in your browser. If Hplay breaks, it offers to send HostLabo a crash report, and only sends one if you press Send Report; the report says which version, which phone and what went wrong, never what you play. Those are the only times it reaches out, and it never uploads your music or anything about it.

Does Hplay need an account?

No. There is no sign-in, because there is nothing to sign in to. There is no account, no subscription, no ads and no tracking of any kind. Your listening statistics are counted on the device and stay there.

Which audio formats does Hplay play?

Whatever the phone itself can decode, which on Android means mp3, flac, m4a and aac, ogg, opus and wav at the least. Cover art is read from ID3 tags in mp3 files, Vorbis comments in flac, ogg and opus, and the ilst atom in m4a.

How does Hplay handle playlists?

A folder is a playlist. Point Hplay at a folder of music and every folder inside it becomes a playlist named after that folder, whatever the tags inside the files say. Renaming a folder renames the playlist, and the order of songs within one can be set by hand and is remembered.

Who makes Hplay?

One person: Iwo Dec, working as HostLabo. It is written by hand, with no framework and no build step, and it is not open source. Hplay is copyright © 2026 Iwo Dec, all rights reserved. You can read the full licence, which is short.

Is Hplay free?

Yes. Free, with no paid tier, no ads and nothing to buy inside it. The twenty demo tracks on this site are free to download and free to use as well.

How do I get Hplay?

Download the APK from How to get it and open it on your phone. Android asks whether you trust an app from outside the Play Store; say yes and it installs like any other. There is no account, no waiting list and no form. It is a beta, so things still move between builds; the changelog says what.

Is Hplay on the Google Play Store?

Not yet. While it is in beta it is an APK you download from this site and install yourself. That is also why the version numbers move quickly and why things change between builds.

What does a scroll wheel player mean?

The main control is a ring you turn with your thumb, not a list you swipe. Turning moves the highlight; spinning faster jumps further; the middle opens what is highlighted; around the edge are back (the hexagon at the top), play or pause, and skip. It is the way portable music players worked before touchscreens, rebuilt as an Android app.

Does Hplay show cover art?

Yes, read out of your own files by the app itself. A song whose file has no artwork is given one of twenty sleeves drawn by the app, and it keeps the same one for good.

Accessibility

A click wheel asks a lot of hands, eyes and ears. So Hplay has a whole screen for making it work for you: Settings › Feel › Accessibility.

Everything below is in the app now, and none of it needs an account or a connection. Hold the middle button on any setting and Hplay tells you what it does before you change it.

Seeing it

Display Size
Normal, Large or Larger. Every word on the screen grows, not just some of them.
Bold Text
Heavier letters everywhere, without making them bigger.
High Contrast
Black and white, at the strongest difference the screen can manage. Turning it off puts back the screen you had, not a bright white one.
Screen
Five looks: Light, Dark, Paper (off-white), Night (warm and dim) and High Contrast. New phones start on Dark.
Font
Noto Sans, Noto Serif or Noto Mono, built into the app, so they look the same on every phone.
Scrolling Names
A long name walks across and back. Off leaves it still.

Hearing it

Speak Selection
Reads the highlighted row out loud as you move, and with Menus and songs it also says each song as it starts. It uses your phone’s own text-to-speech voice, and says so if there is none.
Mono Audio
Both channels into one, so nothing is only in the ear you cannot hear with.
Balance
Left, Mostly left, Centre, Mostly right or Right, for when one ear hears better than the other.
Chimes
Two notes when a new song starts, and two falling ones when the list runs out.
Clicker
Six click sounds for the wheel, with Click Volume at Quiet, Normal or Loud, or no clicks at all.

Holding it steady

Wheel Sensitivity
Precise, Normal or Quick. Precise asks for more turn per row, so a shaky hand does not walk the list on its own.
Hold Delay
Short, Normal or Long: how long a press lasts before it counts as holding.
Haptics
A small buzz for each step of the wheel, at Light, Normal or Strong, or off.
Reduce Motion
Stops everything that moves on its own: the drifting artwork, the turning record, screens sliding in, names walking across.
Volume buttons
They only change the volume, held down or not. Skipping songs with them is a separate choice you turn on.

Finding your way

Ask what it does
Hold the middle button on a setting for a plain explanation of it.
A tour
The first start walks you through the wheel, one press at a time.
Big letters
Scrolling a long list shows the letter you are on, large, in the middle of the screen.
Nothing lost by accident
Anything that deletes asks first, with Keep already chosen.

What is not there yet

Hplay draws its own screen and is steered by its own wheel, and it has not been tested with TalkBack, Android’s screen reader, by somebody who uses one every day. Speak Selection is Hplay’s own way of reading things out until then. If any of this does not work for you, I would genuinely like to know: that is fixable, and worth fixing.

This website

This page aims to meet WCAG 2.2 level AA. Every piece of text was measured against what is actually behind it and clears 4.5:1 for ordinary text and 3:1 for large headlines, against the worst pixel behind each line. Everything you can operate has a name a screen reader can read, a visible focus ring, and a target at least 24 by 24 pixels. Each page has one main heading, headings in order, a skip link and proper landmarks, and works at 320 pixels wide without scrolling sideways. Nothing is conveyed by colour alone, nothing moves by itself, and the marker notes and the newspaper cutting are decoration, hidden from screen readers, that only repeat what the page says in ordinary words.

What has not been done: a test with a real screen reader by somebody who uses one every day, which is the test that counts. The photo descriptions are one person’s opinion of a picture. The demo tracks have no words, so there are no captions. The site has one look, a dark one. This is a one-person beta, checked by a script and not audited by anyone qualified.

Why it exists

I made Hplay because everything now is too much.

Every app wants an account, a subscription, a feed and your attention. A music player should want a folder. So Hplay opens on one screen, with one wheel, and nothing to set up before it plays something.

Simple was the point. Thin was not. Underneath it there are stations built out of what you actually replay, six ways to watch the sound, a theme system that colours the icon on your home screen, and an accessibility screen with real controls in it, not a toggle and an apology. Nothing is in your way. Nothing is missing either.

— Iwo
